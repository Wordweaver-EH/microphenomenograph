1. What is the MPI pipeline?

Microphenomenological Interview (MPI) is a qualitative research method for studying first-person experience. A researcher interviews a participant about a specific moment of experience (in our example studies, an "imaginative suggestion" where the participant closes their eyes and follows an instruction like "imagine your hands are stuck together"). The interview produces a transcript. From many such transcripts, the analyst tries to find patterns.

The methodology used here is the simplified procedure of Sheldrake & Dienes (2025) documented in manual_kev.md. This pipeline automates that procedure using LLM subagents under a strict bookkeeping contract.

The pipeline is a command-line tool exposed as the /mpi slash command in Claude Code. You give it a folder of transcripts and tell it what your study's variables are; it produces a series of analysis artifacts and, finally, candidate mechanism hypotheses for downstream testing — not causal estimates (see the callout under section 3).

Why "documentation-as-done"? The whole pipeline runs on one rule: a step isn't finished until it has produced its output file, recorded what it did in an audit log, updated the manifest, and committed everything to git. There's no "trust me, I did it" — the disk has to show the work or the step is still pending.

2. Starting a run: /mpi init

You start with a folder of transcripts (e.g. p1s1.txt through p7s3.txt — seven participants × three suggestions each). Run:

/mpi init --run runs/my-study/ --transcripts path/to/transcripts/

init does three things, in order:

1. scan_transcripts (mechanical) — Copies transcripts into the run folder. Parses each file's header line (Participant 1, Suggestion 2 (Scored 3/5)) to enumerate participants, suggestions, and scores.
2. propose_study_config (LLM) — Reads a small sample of utterances per transcript and proposes:
  • An Independent Variable (IV) scheme: e.g., "score category: low (0-1), moderate (2-3), high (4-5)".
  • A list of Dependent Variable (DV) foci: e.g., "cognition, emotion, sensations, imagination".
The agent writes its proposal to disk with a rationale.
3. confirm_study_config (you) — Claude shows you the proposal. You accept, edit, or replace it. Your final answer is locked into the manifest. Every downstream step reads from this manifest entry — IV categories scope cross-participant comparisons, DV foci shape what hypothesis generation focuses on.
IV bin rules. IV levels can't overlap in their numeric ranges — overlap is rejected at init with a named error pointing at the offending pair, and the user fixes the bins. (Pre-release: no escape hatch.) Bins also have to cover every score that actually appears in the corpus, or you have to explicitly declare which scores you're dropping via excluded_scores. Example: low 1-2 / med 3-4 / high 4-5 overlaps at 4 and is rejected; correct to low 0-1 / med 2-3 / high 4-5 (the manual's example bins) or similar.

3. The state machine: setup + 7 analysis stages + 1 sidecar check

The pipeline has three kinds of nodes in the state machine, and the table below lists all of them so you can see the shape end-to-end:

  • Setup: init — one-shot at the start. Parses transcripts, proposes IV/DV via LLM, gets user confirmation. Not a recurring analysis stage; runs once and writes the immutable study block.
  • The seven analysis stages in dependency order: transcript_prep → diachronic → synchronic → generic_diachronic → generic_synchronic → global_synchronic → hypothesis. These are the substantive analytic work; each produces files in analyses/.
  • Sidecar check: irr_calibration — runs automatically after the calibration transcript's diachronic and synchronic complete. Not in the main dependency chain (cross-participant stages do NOT depend on IRR being passed); emits a warning by default alongside the main flow. Two modes: soft check by default (warns but proceeds), or hard gate with --strict-irr (blocks cross-participant stages on low/missing IRR).

Each row in the table below names its kind in parentheses:

Updates to the manifest are atomic on a single substep close. Resumability comes from the manifest reflecting only completed substeps; a partially-finished substep stays pending until its close lands.

4. Why substeps matter

Each stage is broken into smaller "substeps" — typically two or three. This matters for two reasons:

  1. LLM atomicity. Each LLM-driven substep is sized to be answerable in one focused call. "Identify temporal moments AND name them AND check for inter-IDU temporal order" would be three jobs in one head; instead the pipeline asks one question at a time.
  2. Resumability. If something goes wrong on substep 3 of 4 for transcript p5s2, the pipeline re-runs only that substep. Substeps 1-2 stay done; other transcripts stay untouched.

The most interesting substep decomposition is diachronic, which mirrors the manual's procedure exactly:

diachronic.criteria_grouping (LLM) — "Read the whole transcript. Group utterances into temporal moments using criteria like 'the utterances talk about feeling their hands move'."
diachronic.criteria_revision (LLM, can loop) — "Look at your groupings. Do any need splitting or merging? Is anything in the wrong moment?" The agent emits a convergence: {decision, reason} field — either "more revision needed" or "converged". The orchestrator dispatches another revision pass if needed, up to 5 times.
diachronic.idu_naming_ordering (LLM) — "Now name each group (e.g., 'Initial Sensation', 'Imagery Onset') and number them in the order the participant experienced them, not the order they were mentioned in the interview."
Important: there is no diachronic.phases substep. The manual explicitly says: "our simplified approach does not include identifying the diachronic structure, and as such does not consider sub-phases of diachronic units." An earlier draft of this pipeline included a phases substep, borrowed from a different methodology (µ-PATH). It has been removed.

Synchronic similarly decomposes into theme_grouping_within_idu → isu_naming → isu_second_level_grouping, iterated per IDU (not per phase — there are no phases).

Split vs merge: the diachronic ↔ synchronic feedback edge

Within diachronic.criteria_revision itself, the revision pass can do both split and merge — the manual is explicit: utterances "grouped by a single temporal criterion actually represent two or more moments" (split), or "two or more moments actually appear to happen concurrently and not sequentially" (merge). Both directions live inside the revision loop already.

The asymmetry is at the synchronic → diachronic feedback edge. The manual only explicitly licenses the synchronic-driven split: "If synchronic analysis has revealed themes within an IDU that exhibit temporal order, then this indicates that the IDU represents more than a single IDU and should be split." The merge direction (two adjacent IDUs whose ISUs reveal they're really one concurrent moment) is not named.

The pipeline supports both directions, but the merge variant is opt-in via --allow-synchronic-merge since it extends the manual's literal wording. The synchronic artifact has two flag fields:

FieldSourceDefault action
temporal_order_within_idu: boolManual-licensed (line 67)Triggers diachronic.criteria_revision re-close → split; idu_split_after_synchronic audit event.
concurrent_with_adjacent_idu: {idu_id, rationale} | nullExtension beyond manualWith --allow-synchronic-merge: re-close → merge; idu_merge_after_synchronic event. Without: merge_candidate_ignored advisory event only.
"Candidate mechanism hypotheses", not causal estimates. The pipeline's final output is qualitative pattern extraction — it generates conjectures about *what mechanisms might explain* the IV-level differences observed across a small sample of interviews. These are inputs for downstream causal testing in a separate study. They are not themselves causal estimates from a hypothesis test, and should never be reported as such. Every hypothesis artifact carries a verbatim disclaimer to this effect; the weak_evidence_review substep flags any unsupported causal language.

5. The four-part "close" — how each step finishes

Every substep finishes via a phased close protocol. The four artifacts (output files, audit events, manifest update, git commit) do not land at the same instant — they land in a known sequence, with each phase emitting its own audit event so failures are fully reconstructable:

  1. close_attempted — close invoked; allocates a close_id (UUID4).
  2. artifacts_validated — pre-checks pass: artifacts exist & validate against schema, upstream prerequisites are done, artifact-path reservation acquired.
  3. audit_appended — per-decision events (IDU codings, etc.) appended to audit.jsonl, all tagged with close_id.
  4. manifest_replaced — manifest atomically swapped via os.replace. The manifest entry records close_id, parent_head_sha, artifact_shas, and expected_action: "commit_created" — but NOT the commit SHA of the same commit it sits inside (see callout below).
  5. git_commit_succeeded OR git_commit_failed — git add + git commit runs. On success, the event carries mpi.git_commit_sha (post-commit, now computable). On failure, the next event is...
  6. manifest_rolled_back (failure path only) — manifest restored to pre-step-4 state; substep stays pending.

A substep is done iff (a) manifest status is done, (b) audit.jsonl contains a git_commit_succeeded event with matching close_id, AND (c) that event's mpi.git_commit_sha resolves to a commit in git log whose tree contains the manifest entry as recorded.

The manifest cannot record the SHA of its own commit. The commit SHA is a hash of the tree, which includes the manifest. If the manifest contained the SHA, changing the SHA would change the tree, which would change the SHA — infinite regression. So the manifest records only the close's identity (close_id, parent_head_sha, artifact_shas); the result (the actual git_commit_sha) is recorded in the post-commit audit event keyed by the same close_id. Tools that need "which commit closed this substep?" do a one-line lookup: jq '.[] | select(.mpi.close_id=="<id>" and .event.action=="commit_created") | .mpi.git_commit_sha' .mpi/audit.jsonl.
#ArtifactWhy
1Output file(s) in analyses/The actual analysis result — a JSON file with the structured output, a Markdown file with the human-readable table, and (for LLM substeps) a prompt.json with the exact prompt + response + model + tokens used to produce it.
2Audit event(s) in .mpi/audit.jsonlOne line per analytic decision, including which IDU/ISU was coded, what confidence, whether it was flagged, what files were produced.
3Manifest update in .mpi/project.jsonThe atomic commit-point inside the close. Source of truth for state (which substeps are done/pending). 4Git commitOne commit per substep close, message format mpi: <actor> <stage>.<substep> <scope> (<N>units <K>flagged). The post-commit SHA is recorded in the git_commit_succeeded audit event (keyed by close_id) — the join across manifest and audit replaces the impossible self-referential SHA.

All four are done by a single CLI helper: scripts/mpi_step.py close. It runs pre-checks (artifacts exist, schema validates, upstream substeps are done), then appends to audit, then atomically updates the manifest, then commits. If any step fails after pre-checks, the manifest is left untouched and the substep stays pending — the next run will retry it.

Prompt capture makes LLM calls auditable and replayable. Every LLM call writes its exact prompt and response to disk. You can re-run the prompt offline against the same model and verify the call's integrity — that the recorded prompt produces a comparable response. Replay does not verify output groundedness: if the model fabricated an unsupported claim deterministically, replay reproduces the fabrication rather than detecting it. Groundedness still requires transcript-span evidence — every IDU/ISU/hypothesis claim citing utterance line numbers, validated against the transcript. That's a candidate scope expansion, not part of v3.16.

5.5. Transcript-span grounding (the anti-fabrication mechanism)

Replay verifies that a recorded prompt produces a recorded response. It does not verify the response is anchored in the transcript. The mechanism that does is mandatory transcript-span grounding:

Every analytic unit emitted by any generative substep — every IDU, ISU, ISU 2nd Level, GDU pattern, generic-synchronic theme, global-synchronic theme, and hypothesis claim — carries a non-empty array:

{
  "idu_name": "Initial Sensation",
  "moment": 1,
  "criteria": "The utterances talk about initial tactile awareness.",
  "utterance_refs": [
    {"transcript_id": "p1s1", "utterance_number": 12,
     "char_start": 0, "char_end": 87,
     "raw_excerpt": "P1: I felt this kind of tacky, sticky feeling in my hands..."},
    {"transcript_id": "p1s1", "utterance_number": 14,
     "char_start": 0, "char_end": 64,
     "raw_excerpt": "P1: Like glue almost, before anything visual came in."}
  ],
  "confidence": 5
}

The helper validates every utterance_refs entry by resolving the transcript_id + utterance_number through transcripts/offsets/<transcript_id>.json, fetching the raw byte range from transcripts/raw/<transcript_id>.txt, and confirming the bytes match raw_excerpt. Failure modes:

  • missing_span_refs — analytic unit has empty/absent utterance_refs
  • span_out_of_range — transcript_id not in manifest, utterance_number missing, or char range outside the resolved utterance bytes
  • span_excerpt_mismatch — raw_excerpt doesn't match the bytes at the named range
  • raw_transcript_mutated — raw file SHA no longer matches the manifest's recorded SHA

Any of these rejects the close. The manifest stays untouched.

Why this matters more than replay. Replay catches "did the model output this exact thing?" — useful but weak. Span grounding catches "is what the model said anchored in the transcript at all?" — the actual anti-fabrication question. A model that fabricates an unsupported claim either (a) cites no spans (rejected: missing_span_refs), (b) cites spans that don't exist (rejected: span_out_of_range), or (c) cites spans whose excerpts don't match the words it claimed to be quoting (rejected: span_excerpt_mismatch). Replay cannot do any of these checks; grounding does all three.

The grounding chain extends through cross-participant stages: a generic IDU's utterance_refs are the union of its constituent per-transcript IDU refs; a hypothesis claim's refs trace back through generic / global artifacts to specific transcript utterances. Any hypothesis is followable, claim by claim, to raw text.

6. The audit trail (the on-disk story of what happened)

Two sources of truth, by domain. Tools that ask "what's done?" query the manifest. Tools that ask "what happened?" query the audit log. Don't conflate them:

  • .mpi/project.json (manifest) is the source of truth for state — which substeps are pending, done, flagged, error right now. Used by anything that decides what to run next.
  • .mpi/audit.jsonl (audit log) is the source of truth for history — what happened, when, by which actor, with what input/output. Used by anything that reconstructs a run, replays decisions, or computes aggregate statistics.

Plus a third file derived from the audit log:

  • audit.jsonl — already covered above. One structured JSON object per line, with fields named after standard observability conventions (ECS / OpenTelemetry). Machine-readable; greppable with jq.
  • reasoning.log — a human-readable view of the same data, regenerated on demand by mpi_step.py render. It's derived from the JSONL, not written separately, so the two can never drift out of sync.

7. Yolo mode: running the whole thing unattended

The pipeline has two execution modes:

  • assisted (default) — Claude pauses after each per-transcript stage and shows you the output, asking "Accept and proceed?"
  • yolo — Claude runs every stage to completion without confirmation. /mpi all --yolo.
Yolo runs strictly sequentially, never in parallel. One transcript at a time; within a transcript, substeps in DAG order; within synchronic, IDUs in numeric order. This eliminates three problems that parallel fan-out introduces: eager-flag races (synchronic flagging IDU 3 for split while IDU 5 is still being analysed), renumbering thrash (a mid-stream revision invalidates downstream work that was running against the old IDU map), and audit-event interleaving across transcripts. Cost: wall-clock time scales linearly with transcripts × enabled stages × IDUs per transcript — there is no single "minutes × N" formula because synchronic alone runs ~3 LLM substeps per IDU per transcript. A rough class estimate for haiku is ~30s–2min per LLM substep; a 21-transcript corpus with ~6 IDUs/transcript averages ~30–60 minutes of LLM time for per-transcript stages plus cross-participant work on top. Acceptable for unattended research runs. Single-stage commands (/mpi diachronic p1s1) are unaffected — they always operate on one transcript by definition.

In yolo, several safety mechanisms make sure the on-disk state remains coherent even with no human in the loop:

MechanismWhat it prevents
Blocked-state audit eventsIf a substep is skipped because its upstream isn't done, a stage_blocked event is emitted. Absence of an artifact alone isn't traceable; the blocked event is.
Run identity (.mpi/run_id)Every run has a single UUID. All audit events for that run share it as trace_id. Resumed runs reuse it.
Dirty-worktree refusalThe helper refuses to close if your repo has uncommitted files outside analyses/ and .mpi/. Prevents accidentally committing unrelated work.
Commit-failure rollbackIf git commit fails after audit+manifest succeed, the manifest is rolled back to its previous state. The next run will see the substep as pending and retry.
Partial-artifact rejectionIf a subagent writes the analysis JSON+MD but crashes before writing the prompt.json, the helper rejects the close. The partial files stay on disk for inspection; manifest stays pending.
Concurrent-close lock.mpi/close.lock serialises closes. Two parallel runs can't corrupt the manifest. Stale locks are auto-reclaimed only if the holding PID is verifiably dead on the same host.
HEAD verificationAt resume, the helper checks that the current git HEAD matches what the manifest's last commit recorded. Mismatch → refusal to close until you resolve.
Artifact SHA256 pinningEvery audit event records the SHA256 of every artifact file it produced. Silent tampering is detectable.
Cascade resetIf you (or the agent) revise an IDU after synchronic analysis revealed a temporal split, the manifest automatically resets all downstream substeps that depended on that IDU's old form back to pending. No analysis ever runs on stale inputs.

8. IRR calibration: an automatic model-quality check

The manual recommends comparing two independent analysts' work on one example transcript using Cohen's κ, and continuing training until κ > 0.6. This pipeline operationalises that recommendation as two automatic IRR checks per run — no user invocation needed:

  1. After the calibration transcript's diachronic completes (its diachronic.idu_naming_ordering closes), the orchestrator dispatches an alternate-agent re-analysis through every diachronic substep, then computes IRR.
  2. After the calibration transcript's synchronic completes (its last synchronic.isu_second_level_grouping closes across all IDUs), the same happens for the synchronic stage.
  3. The calibration transcript is determined by study.calibration_transcript, set at init (default "first"; alternatives "<transcript_id>" or "stratified" — see glossary). With "stratified", both checks fire once per calibration transcript plus one aggregate summary record per stage.

The IRR check is a three-step procedure: align categories → build coincidence matrix → compute four metrics with bootstrap CIs.

Step 1: Category alignment

Primary and alternate raters invent their own labels — primary's "Initial Sensation" and alternate's "First Awareness" might denote the same moment or might not. A fresh mpi-cross-analyst subagent reads both raters' IDU sets (with definitions) and proposes a correspondence:

{"mapping": [
  {"primary": "Initial Sensation", "alternate": "First Awareness",
   "confidence": 0.82,
   "rationale": "both describe the opening tactile moment before any imagery"},
  {"primary": "Hand Movement", "alternate": "Felt Pull",
   "confidence": 0.76,
   "rationale": "both refer to involuntary hand convergence"},
  ...
 ],
 "unmatched_primary": [],
 "unmatched_alternate": ["Surprise Reflection"]}

In assisted mode, you see this mapping in a prompt and can accept, edit, or override. In yolo, it's auto-accepted with an irr_alignment_auto_accepted audit event so the decision is traceable.

This step mirrors what upath's irrStore.ts does (LLM-mediated alignment with user validation) and what Kev Sheldrake did manually when he and Yesesvi had different IDU counts on p3s1 — rename one rater's categories to align with the other's, then compute κ on the union.

Step 2: Build the coincidence matrix

Categories used by only one rater stay in the union. They appear as rows/columns with zero diagonal and nonzero marginal. This is the textbook Krippendorff (2011, eq. 1–6) procedure — not a project-specific hack. If primary used 4 IDUs and alternate used 5, the matrix is 5×5; one of primary's rows is all zeros except where alternate's codes paired against primary's on the same utterances.

Step 3: Compute four metrics with bootstrap CIs

MetricQuestionOperates on
Nominal α (primary)Given the alignment, do raters label corresponding moments the same?Coincidence matrix (post-alignment)
Cohen's κ (secondary)Same as α; matches the manual literallyCoincidence matrix (post-alignment)

Each metric carries a 95% bootstrap confidence interval (N=5000 utterance resamples, per Hayes & Krippendorff 2007). Same bootstrap sample indices are reused across all four metrics within one call, so the CIs are correlated and consistent. Seed is persisted in the JSONL record for reproducibility.

In this example α point=0.78, ci_lo=0.61 → ci_lo ≥ 0.6 → outcome="passed". (If α.ci_lo were 0.42, the same rule would yield outcome="low".) The alpha_pre_alignment = 0.12 makes the alignment step's contribution visible — most of the headline 0.78 came from the LLM aligner mapping primary's "Initial Sensation" to alternate's "First Awareness". A reviewer should ask: is that mapping defensible? The alpha_sensitivity_low_conf_excluded row recomputes α after dropping low-confidence mappings; a large drop between headline and sensitivity α would mean the result depends on shaky alignments.

Important caveat on the literature thresholds. These bright-lines were established for human analysts after training (Krippendorff, Landis & Koch, the S&D manual). Transferring them to LLM-as-rater is by analogy, not evidence. A model that hits α > 0.667 vs an alternate model is more reliable than one that doesn't, but the absolute threshold doesn't carry the same calibration meaning. Treat them as conventions to anchor your reading, not as scientific pass/fail bars.

outcome = "passed" iff α's CI lower bound ≥ 0.6. Note this is more conservative than the modal published practice (which uses the point estimate). Using the CI lower bound is honest about the small-sample-size noise inherent in single-transcript IRR calibration.

  • If passed, cross-participant stages proceed normally.
  • If low, cross-participant stages emit irr_warning but still proceed.
  • With --strict-irr, low IRR becomes a hard error.
Why four metrics rather than one? α and κ measure labeling agreement given the alignment. αU measures segmentation agreement (do raters see the same moments?). ARI measures partition agreement without alignment. They answer orthogonal questions — high α + low αU means "you cut the transcript differently but labeled corresponding pieces consistently"; high αU + low α means "you found the same moments but called them different things". Each diagnoses a different model-quality failure mode.
Why bootstrap CIs? Single-transcript IRR has wide intrinsic uncertainty — α=0.64 with 68 utterances could plausibly be anywhere from 0.42 to 0.79 just from sampling variation. Reporting a bare point estimate overstates precision. Bootstrap is Hayes & Krippendorff's (2007) explicit recommendation; modal published qualitative-coding work skips it, but at this sample size it's the right call. ~30 lines of pure-Python; no external dep.
Why "model-quality" not "training"? The manual's audience is humans being trained as analysts; here the analyst is an LLM. A low α tells you the primary model can't be reliably reproduced — consider swapping models or revising prompts, not "training the model more". Warning-by-default in yolo; opt in to strict blocking.

9. A concrete walkthrough

Suppose you have 7 participants × 3 suggestions = 21 transcripts. You want to compare imagery vividness across score categories (low/moderate/high) per suggestion.

  1. /mpi init --run runs/phase1/ --transcripts data/phase1/
    Claude reads transcripts, proposes IV: score-category {low: 0-1, mod: 2-3, high: 4-5} and DVs: [imagination, cognition, sensation, emotion]. You confirm via prompts.
  2. /mpi all --yolo
    Claude proceeds through every stage automatically, strictly sequentially — one participant at a time, one substep at a time. Each substep's subagent writes its three artifact files and calls mpi_step.py close. Wall-clock scales linearly with participant count (no parallel fan-out); this is by design (see section 7).
  3. After per-transcript stages finish (sequentially, no parallel fan-out), cross-participant stages run sequentially: generic_diachronic per event, generic_synchronic per worksheet, global_synchronic per (generic-IDU × IV), then hypothesis per DV focus plus a global review.
  4. IRR calibration fires automatically as a sidecar after the calibration transcript's diachronic and synchronic — does not block the rest.
  5. Final outputs land in analyses/ (per-transcript per-substep) and hypotheses/ (per-DV).
  6. git log --oneline shows one commit per substep close — typically 100-200 commits for a 21-transcript run.
  7. python scripts/mpi_step.py render regenerates a human-readable .mpi/reasoning.log summarising every decision.

If anything went wrong mid-run, you can re-run the same /mpi all --yolo — the pipeline reads the manifest, sees which substeps are already done, and resumes from the first pending one.

Substep stays pending after a runEither the subagent returned ERROR, or one of the four close artifacts didn't land.Inspect analyses/<participant>-<substep>.* for partial files; check the audit log for a close_aborted or stage_failed event with a reason.

concurrent_close_blocked after 30sAnother close is holding .mpi/close.lock (the manifest+audit+commit critical section).Wait, or if you're sure no other process is running, mpi_step.py unlock --reason "...".

11. Glossary

MPI
Microphenomenological Interview. A qualitative research method for studying fine-grained first-person experience.
IDU
Incipient Diachronic Unit. A discrete temporally-ordered moment in a participant's experience. Produced by the diachronic stage.
ISU
Incipient Synchronic Unit. A theme present within an IDU. Produced by the synchronic stage.
ISU 2nd Level of Abstraction
Higher-level theme grouping multiple ISUs within an IDU. A distinct column preserved throughout the pipeline.
IV
Independent Variable. A study-design choice (e.g., score category) by which participants are grouped for comparison.
DV focus
Dependent Variable focus. A study-design choice (e.g., "cognition", "emotion") that shapes what aspects of experience the analysis emphasises. Not a filter — full transcripts are still coded; foci only guide attention.
Generic IDU
An IDU pattern shared by multiple participants. Produced by generic_diachronic.
Substep
The methodology's natural unit of analytic work, finer than a stage. Each substep closes its own four-part transaction.
Close
The transactional operation that finishes a substep: output artifact + audit event + manifest update + git commit, executed under the close protocol. The four artifacts do not all land at the same instant — the manifest mutation is the atomic commit-point; substep is truly done iff (a) manifest says so AND (b) the named commit SHA exists in git log.
Yolo mode
/mpi all --yolo. Fully automated execution with no human-in-the-loop confirmation. Runs strictly sequentially — one participant at a time, substeps in DAG order — to eliminate races, renumbering thrash, and audit-event interleaving. Wall-clock scales linearly with participant count.
Prompt-capture artifact (schema_version 2)
Per-substep .prompt.json file containing everything needed to replay the LLM call offline: full message chain, sampling parameters (temperature, top_p, top_k, max_tokens, seed, stop sequences), agent definition file SHA, tool call sequence with inputs and results, token usage (including cache hits), vendor request ID. Replay-grade, not audit-only.
Literature thresholds (IRR)
Conventional bright-lines from the methodological literature, surfaced in each IRR record alongside the metric value: α — 0.667 tentative / 0.8 acceptable (Krippendorff 2018); κ — 0.6 (S&D manual) / 0.61 substantial / 0.81 almost perfect (Landis & Koch 1977); αU — same cutoffs as nominal α (Krippendorff 2016); ARI — no bright-line, diagnostic only. Caveat: all calibrated for human analysts after training; transfer to LLM raters is by analogy, not evidence.
Replay artifact
A prompt-capture artifact at schema_version 2 supports being fed to mpi_step.py replay to reconstruct the original API call. The schema captures every knob that affects model output. If the agent definition file's SHA no longer matches what's recorded in the artifact, drift is detectable. Caveat: replay verifies call integrity (same prompt produces a comparable response), not output groundedness — if the model fabricates an unsupported claim, replay reproduces the fabrication. Stronger anti-fabrication = transcript-span grounding (a candidate scope expansion).
Manifest vs audit (sources of truth)
Two SoTs with strict domain separation: manifest is SoT for state (done/pending); audit.jsonl is SoT for history (what happened). Tools deciding next-action query the manifest; tools reconstructing a run query the audit log. Never mix.
Candidate mechanism hypothesis
The hypothesis stage's output. Generative conjecture inferred from qualitative pattern variation across IV levels in a small sample. Not a causal estimate from a hypothesis test; not a result that supports a causal claim. Inputs for downstream causal testing in a separate study. Every artifact carries a verbatim disclaimer.
Calibration transcript
The transcript used for IRR calibration. Set at init via --calibration: "first" (default; first-to-complete), a specific transcript_id (pre-chosen example), or "stratified" (one per IV-level stratum, deterministic seeded sampling). Recorded immutably in study.calibration_transcript.
Pre-alignment α
α computed before the LLM aligner step, treating non-identical labels as disagreement. Always reported alongside the headline post-alignment α so reviewers can see the alignment's contribution. A large gap between pre and post means the LLM aligner is doing most of the work — defensible if alignment confidence is high, suspect otherwise.
accept-head
mpi_step.py accept-head --reason "<text>". The sanctioned audited path for reconciling a HEAD-mismatch (after rebase, cherry-pick, external commit). Records the new SHA in the manifest and emits an accepted_head audit event. Manual edits to .mpi/project.json are never recommended.
Dedicated run repo
Operational requirement: mpi-init --run <dir> should point at an empty directory (or a dedicated study repo), not an active development repo's worktree. A 21-transcript run produces ~100–200 commits, which conflicts with pre-commit hooks, GPG signing, CI triggers, branch protections in an active repo. The helper's dirty-worktree refusal exists in part to prevent accidental nesting.
Transcript-span grounding
Mandatory v1 anti-fabrication mechanism: every analytic unit (IDU, ISU, GDU pattern, hypothesis claim) carries utterance_refs with raw_excerpt; helper rejects close if any ref doesn't resolve or doesn't match the raw bytes. The actual anti-fabrication mechanism (replay only verifies call integrity).
Raw transcript immutability
Raw transcripts live read-only at transcripts/raw/; SHA recorded in manifest; mutations detected at next close. Normalised version lives at transcripts/normalized/ with a unified diff at transcripts/diff/. Offset registry at transcripts/offsets/ maps normalised line numbers to raw byte ranges so every downstream span ref anchors in immutable raw.
Phased close protocol
Six audit events emitted during close, all sharing one close_id: close_attempted, artifacts_validated, audit_appended, manifest_replaced, then git_commit_succeeded (or git_commit_failed followed by manifest_rolled_back). The manifest records close_id + parent_head_sha; the actual git_commit_sha appears only on the post-commit audit event (the SHA self-reference impossibility).
Run lease
.mpi/run.lease: file held for the entire /mpi all orchestration. Prevents two concurrent runs from dispatching duplicate LLM work. Plus per-substep .reservation.json files for safety even within a single run.
Block bootstrap (αU)
Bootstrap CI for αU resamples contiguous utterance blocks (block length ≈ √N) rather than individual utterances. Necessary because αU measures boundary placement on a continuum; naive utterance bootstrap would destroy the contiguity αU is measuring. Naive bootstrap is fine for α, κ, ARI (those operate on per-utterance label assignments, not boundaries).
Cascade-reset tombstones
When a revision triggers cascade reset, affected artifacts are moved to analyses/_superseded/<close_id>/ rather than left in place. A tombstone.json sibling records the cascade source, timestamp, reason. Prevents humans/scripts from opening stale files.
Claim-level evidence (hypothesis)
Each hypothesis claim carries its own {supports, contradicts, ambiguous, n_transcripts, n_iv_levels_covered, uncertainty_language, negative_cases} object, with raw_span_refs on every entry. The substantive audit happens claim-by-claim; stage-level evidence-source coverage is a navigation aid, not the binding contract.
Trace ID / Run ID
UUID identifying a single pipeline run. Shared by every audit event from that run.
Cascade reset
Automatic resetting of downstream substeps to pending when an upstream substep is revised. Ensures no analysis runs on stale inputs.
IRR calibration
Automatic model-quality check after first diachronic and first synchronic. Three steps: LLM-mediated category alignment → union-of-categories coincidence matrix → four metrics with bootstrap CIs. Outcome based on α's CI lower bound ≥ 0.6. Warning-by-default; opt-in --strict-irr blocks.
Nominal Krippendorff's α
Primary labeling-agreement metric on the aligned coincidence matrix. Handles categories used by only one rater as zero-diagonal rows with nonzero marginals — canonical Krippendorff (2011), not a workaround.
Cohen's κ
Secondary labeling-agreement metric on the same aligned matrix. Matches the manual's wording.
Unitizing α (αU)
Krippendorff (2016) extension. Measures whether raters cut a continuum at the same places — segmentation agreement, label-independent. Orthogonal to α/κ which measure labeling.
Adjusted Rand Index (ARI)
Label-permutation-invariant partition-agreement metric. Independent of the alignment decision; useful sanity check.
Bootstrap 95% CI
Per Hayes & Krippendorff (2007): resample utterances with replacement N=5000 times, recompute each metric on each resample, take 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles as the CI bounds. Same indices reused across metrics for consistency. Seed persisted for reproducibility.
Category alignment
LLM-mediated correspondence between primary and alternate runs' category sets (e.g., primary "Initial Sensation" ↔ alternate "First Awareness"). Proposed by a fresh subagent with per-pair confidence + rationale; user-validated in assisted mode, auto-accepted in yolo. Mirrors upath's irrStore.ts approach.
